Safety culture

Your site. Your program. Our people, ready for it.

A general contractor carries the responsibility for what happens on site. When you bring in a labour-only subtrade, you are trusting that the crew arriving will not become a problem you have to manage — that they show up with their PPE, understand the hazards of their own trade, and follow the program you already have in place.

That is the standard we hold ourselves to. Our crews are trained before they mobilise, not after. They attend your orientation, sign into your system, and work to your site-specific plan. If something on site is unsafe, they stop and raise it with your superintendent rather than working around it and hoping nobody notices.

Drywall, insulation and ceiling work carries its own particular risks: working at height off scaffolds and ladders, respirable dust and silica from sanding and cutting, and overhead work in occupied or partially occupied buildings. Those are the hazards we train hardest against because those are the ones our people actually meet.

Certified training

What every crew member carries

Training is completed before a worker is assigned to a job, not scheduled around it. Records are kept on file and can be produced for your safety officer.

TRN-01

Working at Heights

Crew working above the minimum height threshold hold current Working at Heights training, the program Ontario requires before anyone goes up on a project site.

TRN-02

Fall Protection

Harness inspection, anchor point selection, lanyard and lifeline use, and rescue planning. Covered as practical training, not just a classroom sign-off.

TRN-03

WHMIS & Hazard Communication

WHMIS 2015 training on labels, pictograms and safety data sheets. Compounds, sealants and adhesives all carry hazards worth knowing before the lid comes off.

TRN-04

First Aid & CPR

Certified first aid and CPR on the crew, so there is someone who can act in the minutes before help arrives rather than standing by.

TRN-05

Confined Space Awareness

Awareness-level training so crew recognise a confined space, understand why entry is controlled, and know to stop and escalate rather than proceed.

TRN-06

Fire Safety

Extinguisher use, hot work awareness, evacuation routes and muster points. Reviewed against your site's fire safety plan at orientation.

TRN-07

General Workplace Safety

Every crew member receives workplace safety training covering worker rights and duties, hazard reporting, and the right to refuse unsafe work.

On-site practices

How the crew works day to day

SITE-01

PPE Compliance

Crew arrive with their own personal protective equipment and wear it for the duration of the shift. Full list below.

SITE-02

Site-Specific Safety Plans

Before mobilising we review your site safety plan, hazard assessments and orientation requirements, then work to your program rather than around it.

SITE-03

Daily Briefings & Toolbox Talks

A short talk at the start of each shift covering the day's tasks, the hazards attached to them, and anything that changed on site overnight.

SITE-04

Dust Control & Silica Prevention

Drywall sanding and cutting generates respirable dust and silica. We use dust-controlled sanders, vacuum extraction and respiratory protection to keep exposure down.

SITE-05

Scaffolding Safety

Inspection before use, proper access, guardrails and toe boards in place, and loads kept within rating. Crew do not work off a scaffold that has not been checked.

SITE-06

Ladder Safety

Correct ladder for the task, set at the right angle, footed and secured, with three points of contact maintained. The most routine hazard on any interior job.

PPE carried by every crew member

  • Hard hats
  • High-visibility vests
  • Work gloves
  • Safety glasses
  • Steel-toe boots
  • Respiratory protection

Site-specific requirements: if your project calls for additional protection beyond this list, tell us at the pre-start and the crew will arrive equipped.
